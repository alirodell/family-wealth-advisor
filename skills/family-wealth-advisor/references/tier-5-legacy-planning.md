# Tier 5: Legacy / Estate Planning

## Defining markers
- **Triggered by net worth, not current income.** A household can reach this tier through high sustained income (Tier 4 accumulation over time), a liquidity event (business sale, concentrated stock vesting), or inheritance — regardless of what tier their *ongoing* income falls into
- Net worth approaching federal and/or state estate-tax exemption thresholds — confirm current-year figures in REFERENCE.md; federal exemption figures and state exemptions (where states have their own estate tax) move independently and some states, unlike the federal system, have no spousal portability and can include a "cliff" (exceeding the exemption by a small margin pulls the entire estate into tax, not just the excess)
- Because this tier is net-worth-triggered, always evaluate it **independently of and alongside** whichever income tier (1–4) currently applies — a retiree living on modest income but sitting on a large, appreciated estate is squarely Tier 5 for estate planning even if their current income looks like Tier 1 or 2

## Priority order
1. **Foundational documents, if not already in place** — wills with guardianship designations for minor children, durable powers of attorney, healthcare proxies. These matter at every tier, but become genuinely urgent once real assets are at stake
2. **Beneficiary designation audit** — across every retirement account, life insurance policy, and any transfer-on-death/payable-on-death designations; beneficiary designations override the will, so they need to be current and consistent with intent
3. **Engage an estate attorney** — this tier is where DIY planning stops being appropriate; state-specific exemption rules, cliffs, and portability differences make professional drafting non-optional
4. **Exemption-cliff mitigation strategies** (state-specific), where applicable — credit shelter trust provisions, spousal lifetime access trusts (SLATs), and similar structures set up before crossing a cliff threshold are far cheaper than reacting after the fact
5. **Lifetime gifting strategy** — using the annual gift tax exclusion and/or lifetime exemption to move value out of the taxable estate while thresholds and family circumstances allow it
6. **Charitable vehicles**, where philanthropic goals align — donor-advised funds (DAFs) for simpler ongoing giving, charitable remainder trusts (CRTs) for income-plus-eventual-charity structures, qualified charitable distributions (QCDs) from IRAs once age-eligible
7. **More advanced structures** (irrevocable life insurance trusts for existing or new permanent policies, grantor retained annuity trusts, family limited partnerships, dynasty trusts) — situational, and should be scoped with the estate attorney based on the specific goals (liquidity for estate taxes, multi-generational control, business succession) rather than applied by default

## Not automatically relevant
- Nothing in this tier is a "default" — every structure here should be justified by a specific goal (liquidity, control, tax mitigation, succession) that the estate attorney confirms applies. Don't recommend a SLAT or dynasty trust just because a household is high-net-worth; recommend the professional conversation that will determine if one is actually warranted

## Common pitfalls at this tier
- Treating estate planning as something that only matters once a household is "obviously wealthy" — the exemption-cliff mechanics in some states mean a household can cross into taxable territory well before it feels wealthy, especially with concentrated, appreciating assets like a home or business
- Letting beneficiary designations go stale after account changes (a new Roth IRA, a rollover, a new insurance policy) — this is the most common and most easily avoidable estate-planning failure
- Assuming federal exemption portability applies at the state level — many states with their own estate tax do not offer spousal portability, unlike the federal system
- Delaying the estate attorney engagement because current income "doesn't feel like Tier 4 or 5" — remember this tier is about net worth, not income

## Note on income tier interaction
Tier 5 does not replace Tier 1–4 income-tier guidance. A household should be evaluated on both axes: their income tier still governs which accumulation and tax-advantaged account strategies apply, while their net worth independently governs how urgent estate planning is. State both conclusions rather than collapsing them into one.
