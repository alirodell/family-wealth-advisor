# Tier 1: Foundational

## Defining markers
- Household MAGI comfortably below Roth IRA phase-out thresholds — direct Roth IRA contributions are fully available to both spouses
- Marginal federal bracket is typically 10%–12%, occasionally reaching into 22%
- Illustrative range: household income below ~$100k (confirm current-year Roth phase-out thresholds in REFERENCE.md rather than relying on this round number)

## Account priority order
1. **High-interest debt** (credit cards, anything above ~7-8% APR) — pay this down before investing further; the guaranteed "return" from eliminating it usually beats expected market returns
2. **Starter emergency fund** — $1,000–$2,000 or one month of expenses, enough to avoid new high-interest debt from a small shock
3. **Employer 401(k) match, up to the full match** — never leave this on the table; it's an immediate, guaranteed return
4. **Build emergency fund to 3–6 months of expenses**
5. **HSA, if enrolled in a high-deductible health plan** — triple tax advantage (deductible contribution, tax-free growth, tax-free qualified withdrawals) beats every other vehicle at this tier
6. **Direct Roth IRA contributions** — at this bracket, paying tax now (already at a low rate) in exchange for tax-free growth and withdrawals is almost always the right call over a traditional IRA
7. **Additional 401(k) contributions**, beyond the match, if cash flow allows
8. **529 contributions**, only after the above are funded — don't shortchange retirement savings for education savings at this tier; retirement has no loans, education has more financing options

## Not yet relevant (and why)
- **Backdoor Roth** — unnecessary; direct Roth contributions aren't phased out at this income, so there's no reason to add the extra steps and pro-rata-rule risk
- **Whole life insurance** — the tax-deferral benefit that makes whole life attractive at higher brackets isn't worth much at a 10–12% marginal rate; term life covers the actual need (income replacement, dependents) far more cheaply
- **Municipal bonds** — the tax-exemption benefit scales with marginal bracket; at this tier, taxable bonds or a target-date fund's bond sleeve are simpler and usually net more after tax
- **Advanced estate structures** (trusts, etc.) — a will, beneficiary designations, and powers of attorney are sufficient; nothing here yet approaches estate-tax exemption thresholds

## Common pitfalls at this tier
- Being pitched whole life insurance or a high-fee annuity as a "forced savings" vehicle — flag this directly if it comes up; it's a common mis-sell at every income level, but especially poor value here
- Not claiming the Saver's Credit (Retirement Savings Contributions Credit) — a tax credit (not just a deduction) for retirement account contributions, available at this income level; confirm current-year AGI limits, since this is easy to miss and meaningfully boosts the value of contributing
- Contributing to a traditional (pre-tax) 401(k)/IRA by default out of habit, when Roth is usually better at this bracket

## Graduation signal
Household MAGI approaching the Roth IRA phase-out threshold (see Tier 2), or a shift to self-employment income (which unlocks SEP-IRA/Solo 401(k) regardless of tier) — re-evaluate against Tier 2 guidance.
