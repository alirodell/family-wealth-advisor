# Tier 2: Growing Household

## Defining markers
- Household MAGI still comfortably below the Roth IRA phase-out threshold, but income is substantial enough that maxing accounts (not just capturing the match) is realistic
- Marginal federal bracket typically 22%–24%
- Illustrative range: ~$100k–$250k household income (confirm current-year Roth phase-out thresholds in REFERENCE.md — this tier ends where Tier 3 begins, at the actual phase-out line, not a round number)

## Account priority order
1. **Employer 401(k) match**, in full, always first
2. **HSA max**, if HDHP-eligible — still the single best tax-advantaged vehicle available at any tier
3. **Direct Roth IRA, maxed for both spouses** — still available and still usually preferable to traditional at this bracket, though start watching the phase-out threshold as income grows
4. **401(k) contributions increased toward the max**, beyond the match
5. **529 contributions**, with attention to any state tax deduction — check whether the household's state offers one and confirm they're contributing enough annually to capture it in full
6. **Taxable brokerage**, once tax-advantaged space is being used well — this is also where a comfortable emergency fund transitions from "static cash" to a household's first real investment account
7. **Term life insurance sized to actual need** (income replacement, dependents, mortgage) if not already in place — this is the tier where insurance gaps start to carry real financial weight

## Not yet relevant (and why)
- **Backdoor Roth** — still unnecessary as long as MAGI is under the phase-out; don't add pro-rata-rule complexity preemptively, but do flag it as the near-term horizon if income is trending up
- **Whole life insurance** — the tax-bracket math still doesn't favor it broadly at this tier; term life is doing the actual job of income replacement more efficiently
- **Mega-backdoor Roth, S-corp elections, muni bonds** — these become worthwhile as bracket and NIIT exposure increase; premature here
- **Advanced estate structures** — will, beneficiary designations, and powers of attorney remain sufficient for most households at this tier

## Common pitfalls at this tier
- Under-funding the emergency fund while aggressively investing — a household with real savings capacity can max accounts *and* keep 3–6 months of expenses liquid; don't force a false tradeoff
- Missing a state 529 deduction because contributions aren't structured to hit the annual cap
- Carrying inadequate life insurance once there are dependents and a mortgage — this tier often has the largest coverage gap relative to actual need, since income has grown but insurance hasn't been revisited

## Graduation signal
Household MAGI approaching or crossing the Roth IRA phase-out threshold, or crossing the Net Investment Income Tax (NIIT) threshold — move to Tier 3 guidance, and start thinking about the backdoor Roth path (including checking for any existing pre-tax IRA balances) *before* being forced into it mid-year.
